2025 / ROBOTICS

PROJECT 01

A robot to
look after plants.

When my family travelled, we asked my grandparents to care for our plants. I wondered whether a robot could help.

I worked on a mobile plant-care robot with two mechanical arms, a soil-moisture sensor, and a watering system.

ESP32-S3 · K210 vision · 3D printing · AprilTag

Read the project story+

Start with the need

I collected 99 questionnaire responses about plant care and explored what people needed while away from home.

Build and test

I combined a purchased four-wheel base and open-source arm designs with components I modelled and 3D printed, including a soil-loosening tool. I photographed and labelled plants for a recognition model, connected sensors and motors, and worked on the control program.

Change the approach

Recognising the plants directly was unreliable under different conditions. Adding AprilTag markers to the pots made locating them more dependable. I also adjusted the arm design and movement angles after testing.

What comes next

I want to improve recognition in different lighting and move beyond manually starting each care cycle.

2026 / ROBOTICS

PROJECT 02

Keeping flying
fluff on the ground.

Springtime catkins can scatter again as they are swept. I explored a robot that brings spraying and collection together.

A camera locates the target, the robot approaches it, and a water spray helps keep the fluff in place for the brush to collect.

ESP32 · MaixCAM · Computer vision · Mechanical design

Read the project story+

The idea

Combine wetting and immediate sweeping in one small robot, rather than treating them as separate steps.

How it works

The camera tracks the target while a servo adjusts its viewing angle. As the robot approaches, it activates the spray and brush. The prototype brings together a water tank, pump, nozzle, collection box, and sensors.

Next questions

How well can it work when the lighting changes? How could battery life and collection capacity improve? These are areas for further testing and development.

2026 / AI & ACCESSIBILITY

PROJECT 03

Making a phone
less confusing.

A permission request, an advertisement, or a payment screen can leave an older user unsure what to do.

I designed an AI assistant that reads a screenshot, explains the page in plain language, and offers guidance suited to the situation.

OCR · Visual understanding · Workflow design

Read the project story+

A structured workflow

The design uses a 12-node workflow to recognise text and visual elements, classify the situation, and route it to the appropriate response. The output follows three parts: what the page shows, what to watch for, and a suggested next step.

Test the difficult cases

Initial tests covered location permissions, advertisements, payments, ordinary chat, and verification-code screens. For sensitive operations, the assistant encourages pausing and checking with family or an official source.

A lesson in uncertainty

Testing showed that responses could sound too certain. A payment page is not automatically a scam, and an ordinary-looking page is not a guarantee of safety. I want the assistant to communicate that uncertainty more carefully.

2024 / SCIENCE

PROJECT 04

A closer look
at fruit DNA.

Questions about the differences between fruits led me to a smaller first step: learning how to extract DNA.

I explored how fruit type and the conditions of extraction affected the material I could collect, keeping notes and observing samples under a microscope.

Experimental design · Observation · Lab notes

Read the project story+

Make the question testable

With guidance from teachers and family, I learned to change one condition at a time. I compared different fruits, alcohol concentrations, and temperatures.

Learn through observation

I recorded what I saw, photographed samples, and compared changes in appearance. The project gave me a first-hand introduction to experimental work and the limits of simple measurements.

A starting point

This was an early learning project. It helped me practise asking a focused question, planning an experiment, and keeping a record of the process.
